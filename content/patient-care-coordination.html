What patient care coordination means

The Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ) defines care coordination in primary care as deliberately organizing patient care activities and sharing information among all of the participants concerned with a patient's care, to achieve safer and more effective care. In practice, it means making sure the patient, their primary care team, specialists, hospitals, pharmacies and caregivers are working from the same plan.

What care coordination looks like day to day

  • Following up after hospital and emergency visits
  • Making sure referrals turn into appointments, and results come back
  • Reconciling medications and helping with refills
  • Keeping a care plan current and shared with the patient
  • Checking in between visits on symptoms, readings and goals
  • Connecting patients with home and community services

Why it's hard to do for every patient

Care coordination is a lot of small, repeated contacts. A coordinator working office hours can only make so many calls a day, and patients don't always pick up. So practices focus on their most complex patients, and everyone else gets coordination only when something goes wrong.

How an AI care coordinator helps

An AI care coordinator makes the routine contacts for every patient: monthly check-ins, post-discharge calls, referral follow-ups, refill requests and visit booking. It documents each one and hands anything clinical to your team. Your human coordinators and nurses spend their time on the patients and problems that need them.

Frequently asked questions

What is patient care coordination?

Organizing a patient's care and sharing information across everyone involved in it, including the patient, so care is safer and more effective.

What does a care coordinator do?

A care coordinator follows up with patients between visits, after discharges and after referrals, helps with medications and appointments, keeps the care plan current and connects patients to services.

Does Medicare pay for care coordination?

Yes, through programs such as Advanced Primary Care Management, Chronic Care Management, Transitional Care Management and Remote Patient Monitoring, each with its own requirements.
