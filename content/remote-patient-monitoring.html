Why RPM programs lose patients

Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM) pays practices for collecting and acting on readings from devices patients use at home, such as blood pressure cuffs, glucose meters and scales. The program depends on patients sending readings regularly and on someone following up when they don't, or when a reading is out of range. Devices end up in drawers, batteries die and patients forget, and each missed reading puts the month at risk.

What an AI care coordinator does for RPM

  • Notices gaps: when a patient stops sending readings, the coordinator calls or texts them, usually the same day.
  • Fixes simple problems on the call: batteries, pairing, how to take a reading, where to put the cuff.
  • Follows up on out-of-range readings with the questions your protocol sets, and routes anything clinical to your team.
  • Keeps people engaged with a short monthly check-in, so monitoring feels like part of their care, not a chore.
  • Documents every contact for your team's review.

Frequently asked questions

Does the AI care coordinator provide RPM devices?

No. It works with the devices and RPM platform your practice already uses, and focuses on keeping patients engaged and following up on readings.

Can RPM be combined with other care management programs?

RPM is often run alongside chronic care management or APCM. The rules on which codes can be billed together change, so check the current CMS guidance and your billing advisor.
