Referral leakage is common and expensive

A referral only helps the patient if it turns into a visit. Many don't. The American College of Physicians reports that up to half of referrals to specialists are never completed, and in one large health system only about 54% of internal referrals were completed. In dentistry, one national study found 46% of referrals to dental specialists went unfulfilled, and a recent analysis of over 350 specialist practices found the average practice loses over 25% of referrals before a consultation.

The reasons are ordinary: the patient was told to call and didn't, the referral sat in a fax or e-referral queue, or the specialist's office called once and gave up.

How the AI care coordinator closes referrals

  1. The referral comes in. From your EHR, a referral queue or a daily list.
  2. The patient gets a call within hours. The coordinator explains why they were referred, answers simple questions and offers appointment times.
  3. The appointment gets booked. Directly in the specialist's schedule where integrated, or with a confirmed time passed to their front desk.
  4. The loop gets closed. The referring practice is told the patient is booked, and the coordinator follows up if the patient misses the visit.

Who it's for

  • Primary care practices that want to know their referrals turn into visits, which also supports care coordination under APCM.
  • Specialist practices that receive referrals and want every referred patient on the schedule.
  • Dental specialists such as oral surgeons, endodontists, periodontists and orthodontists.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage of referrals are never completed?

The American College of Physicians reports up to half of referrals to specialists are never completed. Rates vary by practice and specialty.

How fast does the coordinator contact referred patients?

Usually within hours of the referral arriving, with follow-up calls and texts until the patient is reached or opts out.

Does it work for dental referrals?

Yes. The same process works for dental specialists who receive referrals from general dentists.
