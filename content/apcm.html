What APCM is

Advanced Primary Care Management (APCM) is a Medicare program that started on January 1, 2025. It pays the practitioner responsible for a patient's primary care a monthly amount for managing that patient's care between visits. CMS describes APCM as a monthly bundle of care management, communication and population health services, billed with three HCPCS codes.

Unlike Chronic Care Management, APCM is not time-based. You bill once per patient per calendar month when the requirements are met, without counting minutes.

The three APCM codes

CodeWho it coversApprox. 2025 national rate
G0556 (Level 1)Patients with zero or one chronic condition~$15 per month
G0557 (Level 2)Patients with two or more chronic conditions~$50 per month
G0558 (Level 3)Patients with two or more chronic conditions who are Qualified Medicare Beneficiaries~$107 per month

Rates vary by locality and change each year with the Medicare Physician Fee Schedule. In 2025, G0557 made up about 80% of APCM claims.

What CMS requires

CMS lists these service elements. They must be provided when clinically appropriate for the patient; not every element is needed every month.

  1. Consent. Get written or verbal consent before starting and document it in the record. The patient must be told only one practitioner can bill APCM in a month, that cost sharing may apply, and that they can stop at any time.
  2. Initiating visit for new patients, billed separately, unless an exception applies.
  3. 24/7 access and continuity of care, including a way to reach the care team about urgent needs at any hour.
  4. Comprehensive care management, including needs assessments, preventive services and medication reconciliation.
  5. A patient-centered care plan that is electronic, maintained, shared and given to the patient.
  6. Care transitions, including timely follow-up after certain discharges.
  7. Coordination with other practitioners and with home- and community-based care.
  8. Enhanced communication, such as secure messaging, email or a patient portal.
  9. Population-level management of the practice's patients.
  10. Performance measurement and reporting.

APCM services can be provided by clinical staff and auxiliary personnel under the billing practitioner's general supervision.

How to launch APCM in four steps

  1. Find eligible patients. Sort your Medicare patients into Level 1, 2 and 3 based on chronic conditions and QMB status.
  2. Set your protocols. Your clinicians define red flags, escalation paths and what the coordinator may handle on its own.
  3. Enroll. The coordinator calls patients, explains the program and records consent.
  4. Run the month. Check-ins, care plan updates and a billing report at month end for your practitioner to review.

APCM or CCM?

APCM bundles elements of Chronic Care Management, Principal Care Management and Transitional Care Management into one monthly code. The same practitioner can't bill APCM and CCM for the same patient in the same month, but can choose which to bill each month.

The bigger difference is how each is paid. CCM pays for minutes of clinical staff time, so it only grows when you add staff, and an AI call doesn't add CCM minutes. APCM pays for managing the patient each month with no minutes to count. That is why we automate APCM, and why it can cover your whole Medicare panel. See CCM vs APCM for the details.

Frequently asked questions

Is APCM time-based?

No. APCM services aren't time-based. You bill once per patient per calendar month when the billing requirements are met.

Who can bill APCM?

Physicians and non-physician practitioners, such as nurse practitioners and physician assistants, who are responsible for all of the patient's primary care and serve as the focal point for their care. It is used mostly by family medicine, internal medicine, geriatrics and pediatrics.

Can an AI do APCM outreach?

CMS lets clinical staff and auxiliary personnel provide APCM services under the billing practitioner's general supervision, and it doesn't count minutes. An AI care coordinator can do the outreach, check-ins and documentation while your practitioner stays responsible and bills. Have your compliance advisor review your setup.

Do patients need to consent?

Yes. You need written or verbal consent before starting, documented in the record. The patient must be told about cost sharing, that only one practitioner can bill APCM per month, and that they can stop at any time.

Do patients need a chronic condition?

No. G0556 covers patients with zero or one chronic condition. G0557 and G0558 require two or more.
