The short version

Most practices that want to run APCM or CCM consider three options: hire care coordinators, outsource to a care management service, or use an AI care coordinator alongside their own team. They differ in how many patients get reached, when, by whom and how you pay.

When hiring makes sense

A human care coordinator is the right choice for your most complex patients: people with many conditions, social needs or frequent hospital visits, where relationship and judgment matter most. Those coordinators get more done when they aren't also making hundreds of routine calls.

When an outsourced service makes sense

A managed CCM service can launch a program quickly if you don't want to manage staff. Check how they're paid: a percentage of collections can raise compliance questions, and their nurses' capacity still limits how many patients get reached.

When an AI care coordinator makes sense

When you want every eligible patient reached every month, including evenings, with your own team handling the clinical part. The coordinator does the routine contacts and documentation, and escalates to your clinicians using your rules.

The combination most practices end up with

The AI care coordinator covers the whole panel with monthly check-ins, consent calls, post-discharge calls and referral follow-up. Your nurses and human coordinators take escalations and focus on the highest-risk patients.

Frequently asked questions

Will an AI care coordinator replace my staff?

No. It takes on routine outreach and documentation, so your clinical staff spend their time on escalations and the patients who need a person.

Do patients accept calls from an AI?

The coordinator says it's an AI calling for your practice, and patients can ask for a person at any time. Check-ins are short, specific and about their own care.
